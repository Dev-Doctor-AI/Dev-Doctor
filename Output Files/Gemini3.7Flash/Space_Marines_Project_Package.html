1.1 Executive Summary

▼

Space Marines is a high-stakes, cooperative first-person shooter (FPS) built for 4-player squad play. Players assume the roles of genetically augmented, heavily armored frontline operators deployed into deep-space industrial facilities, mining outposts, and derelict naval vessels overrun by biomechanical alien swarms. The core gameplay loop focuses on high-density swarm clearance, resource conservation, positional defense, and coordinated retreat through claustrophobic, low-light corridors.

2.1 Kinematic Movement Mechanics

▼

The Marine movement system balances the feeling of immense weight with precise tactical agility.

         |           +---------------------------+           |
         +---------- |       TACTICAL SLIDE      | ----------+
                     |    (7.5 -> 2.0 m/s)       |
                     +---------------------------+
  • Tactical Jog (Base): Forward velocity $V_{\text{max}} = 4.2 \text{ m/s}$; Strafe/Backwards $V_{\text{strafe}} = 3.2 \text{ m/s}$. Acceleration curve: $a = 18.0 \text{ m/s}^2$; Deceleration damping: $d = 12.0 \text{ m/s}^2$.
  • Combat Sprint: Forward-only velocity $V_{\text{sprint}} = 6.8 \text{ m/s}$. Requires sustained input. Drains stamina pool ($100 \text{ units}$) at $15 \text{ units/sec}$. Turn-rate limited by angular velocity cap ($\omega_{\text{max}} = 120^\circ/\text{s}$) to communicate mechanical inertia.
  • Tactical Slide: Initiated by pressing [Crouch] while sprinting. Immediate impulse velocity of $7.5 \text{ m/s}$, decaying non-linearly over $1.2 \text{ seconds}$ to a static crouch ($2.0 \text{ m/s}$). Marine can fire high-dispersion hip fire during the slide.
  • Mantle & Vault: High-speed trace sweeps dynamically identify ledges between $0.5\text{m}$ and $2.1\text{m}$. Root-motion interpolation executes vertical clearance at $2.2 \text{ m/s}$, restricting primary weapon fire during the transition.
  • Inertial Landing Damping: Vertical falls $> 3.5 \text{ meters}$ trigger an impact compression state, reducing movement speed by $40%$ for $0.6 \text{ seconds}$ and inducing procedural vertical camera displacement.

2.2 Armor Integrity & Ablative Plating System

▼

Marine survivability is governed by a two-tier defense model: Ablative Power Armor (APA) and Biological Vitality (HP).

Incoming Damage Vector
         |
         v
+---------------------------------------------------+
| Is Directional Kinetic Barrier Active?            |
| -> YES: Mitigate 75% Damage, Apply Shield Drain   |
| -> NO: Pass to Ablative Plating                   |
+---------------------------------------------------+
         |
         v
+---------------------------------------------------+
| Ablative Armor Remaining?                         |
| -> YES: Absorb via Segment Damage Reduction       |
| -> NO: Pass unmitigated damage directly to HP     |
+---------------------------------------------------+

Armor Damage Reduction Formula

▼

$$D_{\text{applied}} = D_{\text{raw}} \times \left(1.0 - \text{Clamp}\left(\frac{\text{Armor}{\text{current}}}{\text{Armor}{\text{max}}} \times R_{\text{mitigation}}, , 0.0, , 0.85\right)\right)$$

Where $R_{\text{mitigation}}$ is the class base mitigation coefficient (Nominal: $0.65$; Heavy Gunner: $0.80$).

  • Directional Armor Plating: Armor is split into four distinct quadrants: Frontal Torso, Rear Torso, Left Flank, and Right Flank. Attacks from the rear bypass $50%$ of remaining armor points directly to HP.
  • Depletion & Shield-Gate: When an armor quadrant hits $0 \text{ pts}$, a $0.25\text{-second}$ invulnerability window (Shield-Gate) triggers, preventing fatal one-shot damage spikes from high-tier alien attacks.
  • Field Repair: Armor does not regenerate automatically. Players must use Field Welding Torches or pick up Nanite Armor Cores from supply crates.

SLIDE 1: Title Slide

SPACE MARINES

Gritty, High-Stakes Co-op Horde Survival

  • Genre: Tactical Co-op First-Person Shooter (FPS)
  • Target Platforms: PC | PlayStation 5 | Xbox Series X|S
  • Core Vision: An intense, claustrophobic squad survival experience pitting heavy armor and heavy firepower against relentless, high-density alien swarms inside dark industrial corridors.

SLIDE 2: The Problem

The Gap in Co-op Horde Shooters

  • Compromised Swarm Density: Existing co-op shooters routinely cap active enemy counts or retreat to wide-open arenas to manage AI overhead, diluting the visceral dread of fighting an overwhelming biomass in tight spaces.
  • Loss of Claustrophobic Tension: Modern shooters emphasize high-mobility kite mechanics over heavy, grounded squad defense, leaving players without a true dark, corridor-based "last stand" survival horror experience.
  • Superficial Swarm Mechanics: High-count enemy games often sacrifice individual combat reactivity, hit responsiveness, and close-quarters threat dynamics just to keep frame rates stable.
  • The Unmet Desire: Players want the immediate, heavy impact of first-person gunplay and tactical squad coordination against 250+ relentless enemies bearing down narrow industrial chokepoints without technical compromises.

SLIDE 3: The Solution: Your Project

High Concept Pitch

Space Marines is a gritty, high-stakes co-op first-person shooter delivering an intense horde-survival experience. Players don heavy armor and wield overwhelming firepower to mount desperate, back-to-back last stands against high-density alien swarms deep within dark, industrial corridors.

The Core Pillars

  • Visceral First-Person Combat: Heavy, responsive gunplay and immediate physical feedback engineered for close-quarters firefights.
  • Claustrophobic Survival Horror: Dark, narrow industrial environments that cultivate relentless tension, tactical flashlight discipline, and panic-inducing chokepoints.
  • High-Density Horde Warfare: Groundbreaking macro-to-micro horde architecture delivering 250–300 simultaneous on-screen enemies without sacrificing performance.
  • High-Stakes Squad Co-op: Coordinated defensive holding lines, shared firing lanes, and synchronized resource management across PC, PS5, and Xbox Series X|S (Target: 1080p @ 60 FPS).

SLIDE 4: Core Features & Loop

The Core Engagement Loop

  • Infiltrate & Secure: Squads navigate dark, claustrophobic industrial corridors with limited visibility and scarce resources.
  • Hold & Coordinate: Establish defensive choke points and coordinate overlapping fire zones as threat levels spike.
  • Survive the Swarm: Repel 250–300 simultaneous active alien bio-threats through sustained, back-to-back combat.
  • Extract / Push Forward: Clean up surviving close-quarters targets and advance deeper into infested territory.

Key Mechanics & Features

  • Seamless Macro-to-Micro Horde Combat: Flow-field AI navigates massive swarms down tight corridors, dynamically converting to individual NavMesh attack agents within 15 meters for direct melee engagement.
  • Visceral Kinetic Gunplay: Heavy firepower paired with immediate physical responsiveness, supported by client-side ragdoll physics, dismemberment bitmasks, and impact gore.
  • High-Tension Claustrophobic Defense: Tight corridor layouts force rigid squad formations, tactical flashlight/lighting discipline, and relentless choke-point management.
  • Responsive Co-Op Shooting: Low-bandwidth, client-predicted hit registration backed by server-side validation guarantees instant feedback against high-volume target packs.

SLIDE 5: Unique Selling Proposition

What Sets Space Marines Apart

MVP Definition

▼

Summary

The MVP targets a playable 4-player co-op vertical slice on PC that validates the core game loop: a corridor push, an objective lockdown wave defense, and a high-intensity retreat to extraction. It retains the signature weighty kinematic movement, directional quadrant armor mechanics, and baseline 4-class tactical synergy while cutting progression trees, secondary platforms, and multi-biome content.

In Scope

  • ✓ **Core 3-Phase Mission Loop:** One claustrophobic industrial facility corridor map supporting the full "Corridor Push $\rightarrow$ Objective Lockdown $\rightarrow$ Desperate Retreat" cycle with flow-field alien horde pathing.
  • ✓ **4-Class Baseline Roster:** Implementation of Tactician, Heavy Gunner, Demolitionist, and Field Medic with their default weapon kits, passive perks, tactical abilities, and ultimate abilities.
  • ✓ **Weighty Kinematic Movement System:** Marine movement state machine including Tactical Jog, directional Combat Sprint with stamina drain and inertia caps, Tactical Slide with hip-fire dispersion, and vertical mantle/vault mechanics.
  • ✓ **Quadrant Ablative Armor & Health Model:** Directional 4-quadrant armor simulation (front, rear, flanks) with rear-bypass math, 0.25s shield-gate invulnerability windows, and manual nanite/welder repair mechanics.
  • ✓ **PC Steam Multiplayer Baseline:** 4-player networked co-op targeting the PC hardware baseline (GTX 1060 / Ryzen 5 1600).

Out of Scope

  • ✕ **Multi-Branch Class Talent Trees:** Tier 1–3 mastery customization branches (Offensive, Defensive/Support, Utility/Tech) for all classes.
  • ✕ **Console Platform Releases:** Development, platform SDK integrations, and compliance testing for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.
  • ✕ **Multiple Environment Biomes:** Deep-space mining outposts, derelict naval flagships, and dynamic multi-lane map variants.
  • ✕ **Advanced Upscaling Integration:** Vendor-specific temporal upscaling pipelines (NVIDIA DLSS 3.7 / AMD FSR 3.1 frame generation suites).

Core 3-Phase Mission Loop and Flow-Field Horde Pathing

▼

User Stories & Criteria

As a Space Marine squad operative, I want to advance through tight industrial corridors, defend a central terminal during lockdown, and fight through pursuing alien swarms to extraction, so that my squad experiences a complete, escalating tactical mission loop.

Technical Specifications

Phase state is server-authoritative and synchronized via network bitmasks. Alien horde simulation CPU budget must not exceed 3.5ms per frame on the AMD Ryzen 5 1600 baseline.

Baseline 4-Class Tactical Combat Roster (Tactician, Heavy Gunner, Demolitionist, Field Medic)

▼

User Stories & Criteria

As a co-op squad player, I want to select and deploy as one of four distinct baseline classes—Tactician, Heavy Gunner, Demolitionist, or Field Medic—equipped with fixed default weapon kits, signature passives, tactical cooldown abilities, and high-impact ultimate abilities, so that our four-player squad can synergize directional armor defense, crowd control, fire support, and team sustainment during the mission loop.

Technical Specifications

Class state, weapon inventory, ability timers, and charge pools are defined via static data assets (DataTables) with talent trees stripped out for MVP. Networking follows a client-prediction / server-authoritative replication model using Unreal Engine Gameplay Ability System (GAS) concepts: ability inputs trigger local visual prediction cues, while cooldown deducts, projectile spawning, and buff/debuff tags (e.g., 'State.Debuff.TargetMarked') are validated and replicated by the listen-server host at 30Hz minimum. Replicated class struct size is constrained under 128 bytes per player to maintain low network overhead on baseline broadband connections.

Weighty Kinematic Movement System

▼

User Stories & Criteria

As a Marine combat operator, I want a grounded, physics-informed locomotion system with momentum constraints, directional sprinting, tactical sliding, and contextual mantling, so that positioning in claustrophobic corridors feels deliberate, weighty, and mechanically tactical.

Technical Specifications

Implemented via a C++ Character Movement Component sub-state machine (Idle, Tactical Jog, Combat Sprint, Tactical Slide, Mantle/Vault). Kinematic sweep traces (forward forward-box trace 0.8m x 1.2m, downward ledge trace) execute at 60Hz. Networked co-op uses standard client-side prediction with server replay buffers (sending sprint/slide timestamps and compressed input vectors). Dynamic inertia caps limit maximum yaw angular acceleration based on class mass multiplier to preserve heavy armor physical sensation on the GTX 1060 / Ryzen 5 1600 hardware baseline.

Quadrant Ablative Armor & Health Model

▼

User Stories & Criteria

As a Space Marine player, I want incoming damage resolved against four discrete directional armor quadrants (Front, Rear, Left Flank, Right Flank) with shield gating and manual nanite repair mechanics, so that positioning, squad facing, and active field maintenance dictate tactical survival.

Technical Specifications

Directional resolution calculated via 2D horizontal plane dot/cross product: cosTheta = Dot(MarineForward2D, Normalize(AttackerPosition2D - MarinePosition2D)). Damage replication uses bit-packed quadrant flags and uint8 health/armor deltas to minimize bandwidth. Shield-gate state is managed as an authoritative server timer; client predicts armor visual decay but cannot predict gate health invulnerability. Welder tool raycasts against four parented capsule colliders mapping directly to quadrant IDs.

PC Steam Multiplayer Baseline & Hardware Performance Optimization

▼

User Stories & Criteria

As a Space Marine squad leader, I want to create, join, and synchronize 4-player co-op sessions via Steamworks with authoritative state replication on hardware equivalent to a GTX 1060 and Ryzen 5 1600, so that our squad can execute corridor-to-extraction tactical missions at stable 1080p 60 FPS without networking desync or frame spikes.

Technical Specifications

Built on Steamworks SDK v1.58+ using ISteamNetworkingSockets with Valve P2P relays (SDR). Topology is listen-server authoritative (Host Marine simulates horde navigation, flow fields, and hit validation). Quadrant armor updates utilize delta-encoded bitmasks (8-bit quadrant ID + 16-bit fixed-point float for health/armor pool values). Horde network serialization uses hierarchical entity batching (position quantization to 16-bit ints, 8-bit yaw) updating at 15 Hz for distant enemies and 30 Hz for proximate enemies within 15 meters of any Marine. Memory and draw call budgets optimized for Pascal architecture: max 2,200 draw calls per frame, 1080p target rendering with static cascade shadow maps.

1. System Overview & Core Engine Architecture

▼

1.1 Architectural Vision & Engine Framework

The Space Marines engine architecture is built upon a hybrid Object-Oriented/Data-Oriented design pattern leveraging Unreal Engine 5.4 C++ with a custom Data-Oriented Technology stack for dense entity operations. The system isolates high-frequency simulations (horde pathing, steering, and hit detection) into memory-contiguous, Cache-friendly Structures of Arrays (SoA), while player mechanics, tactical abilities, and mission flow leverage a decoupled, event-driven architecture using lightweight Gameplay Ability System (GAS) primitives.

1.2 Subsystem Dependencies and Memory Layout

To satisfy the 16GB total system RAM constraint and AMD Ryzen 5 1600 processing budget, dynamic allocations in the game tick are prohibited. Subsystems register with a central USpaceMarineEngineSubsystem singleton managing linear scratchpad allocators and pre-allocated object pools:

  • Horde Memory Arena: 32MB contiguous buffer housing FMacroSwarmEntity structs (300 max instances at 64 bytes aligned = 19.2 KB active dataset; remaining reserved for spatial hash cells and potential field buffers).
  • Spatial Tile Proxy Grid: 512x512 grid cells (256m x 256m play area at 0.5m resolution), 8-bit cost field, 16-bit integration field, 16-bit packed vector flow field = 1.25MB contiguous SRAM cache footprint.
  • Replication Delta Buffer: 4MB circular ring buffer per client for delta-compression and packet replay validation.

2. High-Density Horde Simulation & Flow-Field Navigation

2.1 Eikonal / Dijkstra Flow-Field Architecture

The macro horde pathing bypasses standard A* per-agent calculation in favor of a 2D tile proxy grid overlaid directly on the corridor geometry. The navigation is divided into two phases: Cost Field Generation (static geometry + dynamic obstacles) and Integration/Vector Field Evaluation solved at 10 Hz.

| Level Collision Mesh  | --> | Static Cost Field Grid | --> | Dynamic Obstacle Layer  |
+-----------------------+     +------------------------+     +-------------------------+
                                                                          |
                                                                          v
+-----------------------+     +------------------------+     +-------------------------+
| Output Vector Field   | <-- | 8-Directional Vector   | <-- | Dijkstra / Eikonal      |
| (X: int8, Y: int8)    |     | Normalization (SIMD)   |     | Wavefront Integration   |
+-----------------------+     +------------------------+     +-------------------------+

Cost Field and Vector Field Generation Algorithm

struct alignas(64) FFlowFieldTile {
    static constexpr uint32_t GRID_DIM = 512;
    static constexpr float CELL_SIZE = 50.0f; // 50cm per cell

    uint8_t  CostField[GRID_DIM * GRID_DIM];          // 0xFF = Impassable, 1-254 = Cost
    uint16_t IntegrationField[GRID_DIM * GRID_DIM];   // Distance potential
    int8_t   VectorFieldX[GRID_DIM * GRID_DIM];       // Normalized direction [-127, 127]
    int8_t   VectorFieldY[GRID_DIM * GRID_DIM];
};

void UpdateIntegrationField(FFlowFieldTile& Grid, const TArray<FVector2D>& TargetPositions) {
    std::fill(std::begin(Grid.IntegrationField), std::end(Grid.IntegrationField), 0xFFFF);
    
    // Min-Heap priority queue driven by a circular bucket array
    TArray<uint32_t> OpenQueue;
    OpenQueue.Reserve(4096);

    for (const FVector2D& Target : TargetPositions) {
        uint32_t CellX = FMath::Clamp(static_cast<uint32_t>(Target.X / Grid.CELL_SIZE), 0u, Grid.GRID_DIM - 1);
        uint32_t CellY = FMath::Clamp(static_cast<uint32_t>(Target.Y / Grid.CELL_SIZE), 0u, Grid.GRID_DIM - 1);
        uint32_t CellIndex = CellY * Grid.GRID_DIM + CellX;
        
        Grid.IntegrationField[CellIndex] = 0;
        OpenQueue.Add(CellIndex);
    }

    // 8-neighbor offsets and base distances (fixed point 1.0 and 1.414)
    static const int32_t DX[8] = { 0, 1, 0, -1, 1, -1, 1, -1 };
    static const int32_t DY[8] = { 1, 0, -1, 0, 1, 1, -1, -1 };
    static const uint16_t StepCost[8] = { 10, 10, 10, 10, 14, 14, 14, 14 };

    int32_t Head = 0;
    while (Head < OpenQueue.Num()) {
        uint32_t Current = OpenQueue[Head++];
        uint32_t CurX = Current % Grid.GRID_DIM;
        uint32_t CurY = Current / Grid.GRID_DIM;
        uint16_t CurCost = Grid.IntegrationField[Current];

        for (int32_t i = 0; i < 8; ++i) {
            int32_t NX = static_cast<int32_t>(CurX) + DX[i];
            int32_t NY = static_cast<int32_t>(CurY) + DY[i];

            if (NX >= 0 && NX < Grid.GRID_DIM && NY >= 0 && NY < Grid.GRID_DIM) {
                uint32_t NeighborIdx = NY * Grid.GRID_DIM + NX;
                uint8_t TerrainCost = Grid.CostField[NeighborIdx];
                if (TerrainCost == 0xFF) continue;

                uint16_t NewCost = CurCost + StepCost[i] + TerrainCost;
                if (NewCost < Grid.IntegrationField[NeighborIdx]) {
                    Grid.IntegrationField[NeighborIdx] = NewCost;
                    OpenQueue.Add(NeighborIdx);
                }
            }
        }
    }
}

2.2 SIMD / AVX2 Steering and Flocking Pipeline (30 Hz Tick)

Macro entities update positions via an AVX2-optimized structure-of-arrays (SoA) evaluation loop executing within a 3.5ms CPU budget on the AMD Ryzen 5 1600:

struct alignas(32) FMacroSwarmSoA {
    float PosX[320];
    float PosY[320];
    float PosZ[320];
    float VelX[320];
    float VelY[320];
    float VelZ[320];
    uint16_t EntityID[320];
    uint8_t  StateFlags[320]; // 0x01: Active, 0x02: Promoted, 0x04: Stunned
};

void ExecuteFlockingSIMD(FMacroSwarmSoA& Swarm, const FFlowFieldTile& Grid, float DeltaTime, int32_t EntityCount) {
    __m256 vDT = _mm256_set1_ps(DeltaTime);
    __m256 vMaxSpeed = _mm256_set1_ps(650.0f); // 6.5 m/s swarm rush speed
    __m256 vCellInv = _mm256_set1_ps(1.0f / Grid.CELL_SIZE);

    for (int32_t i = 0; i < EntityCount; i += 8) {
        __m256 pX = _mm256_load_ps(&Swarm.PosX[i]);
        __m256 pY = _mm256_load_ps(&Swarm.PosY[i]);
        
        // Convert World Position to Grid Indices
        __m256 gX = _mm256_floor_ps(_mm256_mul_ps(pX, vCellInv));
        __m256 gY = _mm256_floor_ps(_mm256_mul_ps(pY, vCellInv));
        
        // Vector field sampling and linear integration...
        __m256 vX = _mm256_load_ps(&Swarm.VelX[i]);
        __m256 vY = _mm256_load_ps(&Swarm.VelY[i]);
        
        pX = _mm256_fmadd_ps(vX, vDT, pX);
        pY = _mm256_fmadd_ps(vY, vDT, pY);
        
        _mm256_store_ps(&Swarm.PosX[i], pX);
        _mm256_store_ps(&Swarm.PosY[i], pY);
    }
}

2.3 Macro-to-Micro Promotion System (<15m Dynamic Transition)

  • Promotion Trigger: When Euclidean distance |EntityPos - PlayerPos| < 1500.0f (15m), the entity state flag shifts from Macro to MicroNavMeshAgent.
  • Micro Promotion Routine:
    1. Entity index removed from FMacroSwarmSoA active SIMD buffer.
    2. An instance from the pre-allocated AAlienWarriorMicroPool (capacity: 64 actors) is assigned.
    3. Transforms, linear velocities, and current blackboard states are copied.
