1.0 PROJECT OVERVIEW
Space Marines is a gritty, high-stakes co-op first-person shooter (FPS) designed to deliver an intense, claustrophobic experience. Players take on the roles of elite squads tasked with holding the line against overwhelming and relentless alien swarms. The project emphasizes heavy firepower and visceral combat, capturing the "last stand" energy of fighting back-to-back in dark, infested industrial environments.

1.1 PROJECT IDENTITY AND CORE VISION
The core vision for Space Marines is to create a physical and heavy combat experience. The identity of the game is defined by its industrial sci-fi aesthetic and survival horror undertones. Success is measured by the player's ability to maintain composure while facing massive numbers of enemies in tight, high-pressure scenarios.

1.2 GENRE AND INDUSTRIAL SCI-FI THEME
Classified as a Survival Horror FPS, Space Marines utilizes an industrial sci-fi theme. Environments are characterized by cold metal, hissing steam, and flickering lights. The setting serves as a character itself, providing a sense of dread as threats emerge from ceiling vents, floor grates, and the deep shadows of derelict corridors.

2.0 GAMEPLAY PILLARS
The gameplay experience of Space Marines is built upon four foundational pillars: Visceral Combat, Claustrophobic Terror, High-Density Horde Survival, and Tactical Cooperation. These pillars ensure that every mechanic reinforces the feeling of being an elite but vulnerable soldier in a hostile environment.

2.1 VISCERAL COMBAT AND GUNPLAY SYSTEMS
Gunplay in Space Marines is designed to feel heavy and physical. Every shot has significant impact, supported by a robust gore and dismemberment system. Weapons are tools of survival, and the feedback from firing into a swarm provides a satisfying sense of power and desperation.

2.2 CLAUSTROPHOBIC TERROR AND ENVIRONMENTAL TENSION
The game leverages tight, dark, and industrial environments to create constant tension. The threat is omnidirectional, with enemies capable of emerging from various environmental points like vents and grates. This forces players to constantly scan their surroundings and manage their limited light sources.

2.3 HIGH-DENSITY HORDE SURVIVAL MECHANICS
Space Marines pushes the boundaries of enemy density, with players facing 250–300 simultaneous enemies. Surviving these massive numbers requires players to utilize heavy firepower and maintain tactical positioning to avoid being completely overrun by the relentless alien mass.

2.4 TACTICAL COOPERATION AND SQUAD COHESION
Success in Space Marines depends on tight teamwork. Squad members must cover each other's flanks, manage shared resources, and utilize the environment to funnel the swarm into kill zones. Cohesion is the difference between a successful extraction and a total squad wipe.

3.0 AI AND SWARM ARCHITECTURE
The AI system in Space Marines uses a tiered approach to manage the 300-enemy budget efficiently. By separating logic based on proximity to the player, the game can simulate massive swarms without sacrificing individual combat reactivity.

3.1 MACRO SWARM SIMULATION: FLOW-FIELDS AND FLOCKING
For enemies at a distance greater than 15 meters, the game uses a data-oriented flow-field and flocking simulation. This allows the alien swarm to navigate complex corridors and "flow" toward the players as a singular, terrifying mass while minimizing CPU overhead.

3.2 MICRO COMBAT AGENTS: NAVMESH AND ROOT-MOTION
Once an enemy is within 15 meters of a player, it is "promoted" to an individual NavMesh agent. These agents feature dedicated hitboxes for precise hit registration, root-motion attacks for physical reactivity, and local avoidance to maintain realistic spacing during a charge.

3.3 THE AI DIRECTOR: PACING AND RESOURCE PLACEMENT
The Director is an overarching system that manages swarm density and the placement of light sources, destructible cover, and ammo caches. It monitors player performance to maintain a perfect balance of tension and relief, ensuring the pacing remains engaging throughout the mission.

4.0 LEVEL DESIGN AND GENERATION
Space Marines utilizes a hybrid environment system to balance replayability with high-fidelity visuals. This approach combines procedural generation with hand-crafted tactical spaces to create a cohesive and unpredictable world.

4.1 HYBRID PROCEDURAL GENERATION LOGIC (GRAPH-BASED AND WFC)
The level generation uses Graph-Based Grammar to ensure mission flow and pacing, while Wave Function Collapse (WFC) is used to fill interstitial corridors and junctions. This ensures that every level layout is logical for gameplay while remaining visually diverse.

4.2 MODULAR TILES AND SOCKET METADATA
The environment is built from hand-authored modular tiles, including corridors, vents, and junctions. These tiles feature "Socket" metadata, allowing for seamless snapping and ensuring that the procedural generator creates valid, navigable paths for both players and AI.

4.3 HERO ROOMS AND TACTICAL SET-PIECES
Anchoring key mission milestones are "Hero Rooms"—large, hand-crafted tactical set-pieces. These areas are designed for cinematic defensive stands, providing players with specific environmental advantages and challenges during the mission's most intense moments.

4.4 RUNTIME INTEGRATION: NAVMESH STITCHING AND OCCLUSION PORTALS
During level generation, the system performs NavMesh Stitching using pre-baked shards within each tile. It also links occlusion portals to ensure seamless AI navigation and optimized rendering performance, allowing the engine to handle complex layouts at runtime.

5.0 TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS
Space Marines is developed for PC, PlayStation 5, and Xbox Series X|S. The technical goal is to provide a high-performance experience that can handle the massive enemy counts and atmospheric lighting required by the core vision.

5.1 TARGET PLATFORMS AND PERFORMANCE BASELINES
The target performance is 1080p @ 60 FPS utilizing DLSS/FSR temporal upscalers. The PC minimum baseline is set at an NVIDIA GTX 1060 (6GB), AMD Ryzen 5 1600, and 16GB of RAM to ensure accessibility while maintaining visual fidelity.

5.2 RENDERING PIPELINE: CLUSTERED FORWARD SHADING
The game utilizes a Clustered Forward Shading pipeline. This is optimized for high-frequency dynamic lights, such as muzzle flashes and flashlights, which are essential for the industrial horror atmosphere and the constant combat feedback.

5.3 HORDE RENDERING: VATS AND COMPUTE-DRIVEN LODS
To render hundreds of enemies efficiently, Space Marines uses GPU-instanced Vertex Animation Textures (VATs) and compute-driven skeletal Level of Detail (LOD) systems. This offloads the animation and mesh processing to the GPU, enabling the 300-enemy swarm.

5.4 HYBRID PROBE-BASED GLOBAL ILLUMINATION
Lighting is handled via a Hybrid Probe-Based GI system. Pre-baked light probe data within modular tiles allows for atmospheric, high-contrast industrial lighting without the prohibitive performance cost of full real-time global illumination.

6.0 NETWORKING AND SYNCHRONIZATION
Networking in Space Marines is designed to handle high-density enemy synchronization across a co-op squad. The system prioritizes responsiveness for players while maintaining a synchronized view of the massive alien swarm.

6.1 CLIENT-HOSTED TOPOLOGY AND SERVER AUTHORITY
The game uses a client-hosted listen-server architecture. This eliminates dedicated server costs. The host handles the server-authoritative flow-field simulation, ensuring that the swarm's macro behavior is consistent for all players in the session.

6.2 BANDWIDTH MANAGEMENT AND SPATIAL LOD REPLICATION
Bandwidth is managed through Tiered Spatial LOD replication. Proximal enemies are replicated via quantized 20Hz state snapshots, while distant enemies are driven client-side via synchronized deterministic flocking vectors and sparse state events.

6.3 HIT REGISTRATION AND SERVER-AUTHORITATIVE VALIDATION
Hit registration uses client-side prediction for immediate feedback, with server-authoritative validation. The server verifies range, line-of-sight (LOS), and rate-of-fire to ensure fair play and consistent combat results across the network.

6.4 COSMETIC OFFLOADING AND CLIENT-SIDE PHYSICS SIMULATION
To save CPU cycles on the host, all ragdoll physics, gibbing, and blood particle effects are handled purely on the local client. These are cosmetic simulations triggered by validated hit payloads containing limb-state bitmasks.

7.0 AUDIO AND ATMOSPHERE
The audio and atmosphere of Space Marines are critical for immersion. The system is designed to convey the scale of the swarm while maintaining the intimate, terrifying feel of an industrial horror setting.

7.1 SPATIAL AUDIO OPTIMIZATION AND PRIORITIZATION
The audio engine manages the high density of concurrent sound events from the 300-count swarm by prioritizing sounds based on proximity and threat level. This ensures that the most relevant audio cues, like an enemy flanking from a vent, are always audible.

7.2 ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING AND HIGH-CONTRAST LIGHTING
Space Marines uses a procedural lighting-rig to enforce a strict darkness-to-light ratio. This ensures that even randomized layouts maintain the high-contrast, industrial horror aesthetic that is essential to the brand's identity and environmental storytelling.

### 4. Key Components

#### **WeaponFeedbackController (Client-Side)**
Manages the "feel" of the gunplay. It calculates the procedural recoil using a spring-damper system and scales screen shake based on the `SustainedFireTimer`. It also handles the dynamic audio pitch shifting for "Heavy" tagged weapons.

#### **DismembermentManager (Server-Side Logic)**
Calculates whether a hit results in a limb detachment. It checks the `DismemberChance` and `OverkillThreshold`. Upon a successful trigger, it instructs the `LimbIntegrityMap` to update and sends a `Net_ApplyDismember` RPC.

#### **HordeMeshComponent (GPU Instanced)**
A specialized rendering component for the 300-enemy system. It must support "Mesh Swapping" or "Alpha Masking" via vertex shaders to hide severed limbs on instanced meshes without breaking batching significantly.

#### **ImpactProcessor**
A utility class that translates Raycast/Projectile hits into specific `LimbType` hits by checking the hit-box metadata of the enemy's physics asset.

---

### 5. Logic Flow

#### **Weapon Firing & Feedback Loop**
1.  **Input:** Player holds "Fire".
2.  **Feedback Calculation:**
    *   Apply `RecoilProfile` to camera.
    *   If `WeaponTag == Heavy`, increment `SustainedFireTimer`.
    *   Scale `ActiveScreenShake` by `SustainedFireTimer * 0.5`.
    *   Adjust SFX Pitch: `BasePitch - (SustainedFireTimer * 0.05)`.
3.  **Haptics:** Send vibration command to `ControllerHardware` based on `HapticIntensity`.

#### **Dismemberment & AI Impact Logic**
```csharp
// High-level logic for processing a hit on an enemy
public void ProcessHit(HitResult hit, WeaponData weapon) {
    Enemy enemy = hit.Actor.GetComponent<Enemy>();
    LimbType hitLimb = hit.HitBox.LimbType;
    
    // Check for Torso Overkill
    if (hitLimb == LimbType.Torso && hit.Damage > weapon.OverkillThreshold) {
        TriggerTorsoExplosion(enemy);
        return;
    }

    // Check for Limb Dismemberment
    float roll = Random.Range(0f, 1f);
    if (roll <= weapon.DismemberChance) {
        DetachLimb(enemy, hitLimb);
        UpdateAIStatus(enemy, hitLimb);
    }
}

public void UpdateAIStatus(Enemy enemy, LimbType lostLimb) {
    if (lostLimb == LimbType.Leg_L || lostLimb == LimbType.Leg_R) {
        enemy.MovementSpeed *= 0.4f; // 60% reduction
        enemy.AnimationController.SetState("Limping");
    } 
    else if (lostLimb == LimbType.Arm_R) {
        enemy.DropWeapon();
        enemy.AIBehavior.SetState("Desperation");
    }
}
```

### 4. Key Components/Classes

*   **`TierManager`:** The orchestrator that monitors the distance between all 500+ units and the 4 players. It triggers the component swapping (disabling Flow-Field movement, enabling NavMeshAgent).

### 5. Logic Flow

#### A. Swarm Movement (Flow-Field)
1.  **Target Selection:** The system identifies player positions and designates them as "Goals" (value 0) in the Integration Map.
2.  **Dijkstra Fill:** Starting from Goals, the system populates the Integration Map by adding cell costs to neighbor values.
3.  **Vector Generation:** For each cell, the system compares neighbor values to create a directional vector pointing to the lowest cost neighbor.
4.  **Agent Update:** 500+ units read the vector of their current cell and apply a force to their position.

#### B. Tier Transition Logic
1.  **Proximity Check:** Every $N$ frames, the `TierManager` calculates the distance between a unit and the closest player.
2.  **Promotion:** If distance < 15m AND `ActiveEliteCount` < MaxCap, the unit is "Promoted." 
    *   Disable Flow-Field influence.
    *   Enable `NavMeshAgent` and `EliteTacticalBrain`.
    *   Switch to high-fidelity animation rig.
3.  **Demotion:** If distance > 20m (15m + 5m Hysteresis), the unit is "Demoted" back to the swarm to save CPU.

#### C. Elite Combat (NavMesh)
1.  **Threat Assessment:** The Brain checks `HealthPoints`. If < 50%, it triggers the `SeekCover` state.
2.  **Pathfinding:** The unit queries the NavMesh for a path to a "Cover" node that is not in the player's line of sight.
3.  **Flanking:** If the player is stationary, the agent calculates a destination 90 degrees offset from the player's forward vector, using the NavMesh to prioritize paths with "Low Visibility" tags.

### Key Components/Classes

1.  **HordeManager (MonoBehaviour/System):**
    *   The central orchestrator. It holds the CPU-side array of instance data and manages the lifecycle of the GPU buffers.
    *   Responsible for calling `Graphics.DrawMeshInstancedIndirect`.

2.  **HordeCulling (Compute Shader):**
    *   A kernel that runs 300 threads (one per enemy).
    *   Performs Frustum Culling: Checks the instance position against the 6 camera planes.
    *   Performs Distance Culling/LOD: Calculates distance to camera and appends the instance index to the appropriate LOD buffer (LOD0, LOD1, or LOD2).

3.  **HordeInstanceShader (HLSL Shader):**
    *   A custom shader using `#pragma instancing_options procedural:setup`.
    *   Fetches `ObjectToWorld` and `AnimationOffset` from the Structured Buffer using the `unity_InstanceID`.
    *   Implements vertex animation texture (VAT) sampling to handle 300 animated meshes without the overhead of a standard Skinned Mesh Renderer.

4.  **LODConfiguration (ScriptableObject):**
    *   Defines the meshes used for each LOD level and the distance at which they swap.

---

### Logic Flow

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   `HordeManager` allocates a `StructuredBuffer` of size 300.
    *   The `ArgsBuffer` is initialized with 5 integers per LOD (IndexCount, InstanceCount, StartIndex, BaseVertex, StartInstance).

2.  **Frame Update (CPU):**
    *   AI systems update enemy positions.
    *   `HordeManager` updates the `ObjectToWorld` matrices in the local array.
    *   `HordeManager` uploads the array to the GPU using `ComputeBuffer.SetData`.

3.  **Culling Phase (GPU - Compute Shader):**
    *   The CPU dispatches the Culling Compute Shader.
    *   For each instance:
        *   If (Position outside Frustum) OR (Distance > MaxCullDistance): **Discard**.
        *   Else: Calculate LOD based on distance.
        *   Use `AppendBuffer.Append(instanceID)` to add the enemy to the list of visible units for that specific LOD.

4.  **Argument Copy (GPU - Compute Shader):**
    *   A secondary small kernel copies the `Count` from the `AppendBuffer` directly into the `ArgsBuffer`. This ensures the CPU never has to "read back" how many enemies are visible, preventing a sync point/stall.

5.  **Draw Phase (GPU):**
    *   `HordeManager` calls `DrawMeshInstancedIndirect`.
    *   The GPU reads the instance count directly from the `ArgsBuffer` and renders only the visible enemies using the specified LOD meshes.

### Logic Implementation: Culling Kernel
```hlsl
// Simplified logic for the Culling Compute Shader
#pragma kernel CSMain

struct InstanceData {
    float4x4 matrix;
    float animOffset;
    float3 tint;
    uint isActive;
};

StructuredBuffer<InstanceData> instanceBuffer;
AppendStructuredBuffer<uint> visibleIndicesLOD0;
float4 frustumPlanes[6];
float3 cameraPosition;

[numthreads(64, 1, 1)]
void CSMain (uint3 id : SV_DispatchThreadID) {
    if (id.x >= maxInstances) return;
    
    InstanceData data = instanceBuffer[id.x];
    if (data.isActive == 0) return;

    float3 pos = data.matrix._m03_m13_m23;
    
    // 1. Frustum Culling
    for (int i = 0; i < 6; i++) {
        if (dot(frustumPlanes[i].xyz, pos) + frustumPlanes[i].w < -radius) {
            return; // Culled
        }
    }

    // 2. Distance Check
    float dist = distance(pos, cameraPosition);
    if (dist < lod0Threshold) {
        visibleIndicesLOD0.Append(id.x);
    }
    // Repeat for LOD1, LOD2...
}
```

## 4. Key Components

### WFCManager (Singleton)
The central orchestrator. It initializes the grid, triggers the collapse steps, and manages the lifecycle of the generation process.

### ConstraintSolver
The logic engine that compares sockets. It maintains the "Possibility Space" for each cell. When a neighbor collapses, the solver prunes invalid tiles from adjacent cells.

### TileRegistry
A ScriptableObject (or JSON database) containing all `TileData`. It provides the `WFCManager` with the available pool of assets and their adjacency rules.

### PathValidator
A post-generation utility that uses A* pathfinding to ensure the "Spawn" and "Objective" tiles are connected. It also identifies "islands" (unreachable rooms) for removal or adjustment.

### PropDecorator
Responsible for the "Aesthetic Variation" and "Tactical Cover" requirements. Once the layout is fixed, it iterates through tiles to swap mesh variants and spawn cover objects (crates, barrels) based on the `CoverPoints` data.

## 5. Logic Flow

The generation follows a standard WFC pattern with a custom validation pass for gameplay.

### Phase 1: Collapse
1.  **Initialize:** Create a 3D grid where every cell contains a list of all possible `TileIDs`.
2.  **Pick Seed:** Select a random cell (usually the center or a designated Spawn cell) and collapse it to a single random TileID.
3.  **Propagate:** 
    *   Check neighbors of the collapsed cell.
    *   Remove any TileIDs from neighbors that don't match the sockets of the collapsed cell.
    *   If a neighbor's possibility list changes, add its neighbors to a stack to propagate further.
4.  **Iterate:** 
    *   Find the cell with the lowest "Entropy" (fewest remaining possibilities).
    *   Collapse it.
    *   Propagate.
    *   Repeat until all cells are collapsed or a **Contradiction** (0 possibilities) is found.
5.  **Backtrack/Restart:** On contradiction, clear the grid and restart with a new internal seed (up to a max retry limit).

### Phase 2: Gameplay Validation
```csharp
// High-level Logic for Connectivity Check
public bool IsLevelPlayable(Grid grid) {
    var start = grid.FindTileWithFlag(IsSpawnPoint);
    var end = grid.FindTileWithFlag(IsObjective);
    
    // Use A* to find a path through "Walkable" sockets
    var path = Pathfinding.FindPath(start, end);
    
    if (path == null) return false; // Triggers a restart of Phase 1

    // Identify unreachable clusters
    var allReachable = Pathfinding.FloodFill(start);
    foreach (var cell in grid.AllCells) {
        if (!allReachable.Contains(cell) && cell.Type != TileType.Solid) {
            cell.ConvertToSolid(); // Fill "Islands" with hull/rock
        }
    }
    return true;
}
```

### Phase 3: Visual Finalization
1.  **Mesh Selection:** For each collapsed cell, pick a random `MeshVariant` from the `TileData`.
2.  **Cover Spawning:** Iterate through `CoverPoints`. Use the AI Director's "Line of Sight" rules to place crates that break long hallways or provide flanking positions in hubs.
3.  **Baking:** Trigger static batching for the modular meshes to optimize GPU performance.

### 4. Key Components / Classes

*   **LightingManager (Singleton):** The central authority that collects all light data every frame. It manages the lifecycle of muzzle flashes and maintains the master `LightBuffer`.
*   **Froxelizer (Compute Shader):** Responsible for dividing the camera frustum into 3D clusters (froxels) based on the current View/Projection matrices.
*   **LightCuller (Compute Shader):** Performs the intersection tests between the active lights (spheres/cones) and the froxel AABBs (Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes). It outputs a `LightIndexBuffer` and a `LookupTexture`.
*   **FlashlightController:** A component attached to the Player Prefab that handles input and interpolates the light's rotation to give it "weight" (lag/smooth-follow).
*   **MuzzleFlashPooler:** A memory-efficient pooling system that recycles LightIDs to prevent allocation spikes during high-ROF (Rate of Fire) combat.

### 5. Logic Flow

#### A. Light Culling & Binning (The Clustered Step)
1.  **CPU Phase:** Collect all active Flashlights and Muzzle Flashes. Sort them by priority (Distance to Player > Intensity).
2.  **GPU Phase 1 (Cluster Generation):** If the camera FOV or Aspect Ratio changes, recalculate the AABBs for every froxel in the grid.
3.  **GPU Phase 2 (Culling):** Run a Compute Shader where each thread represents a froxel.
    *   Iterate through the global light list.
    *   Test intersection between the light volume and the froxel.
    *   Store the indices of intersecting lights in a global `LightIndexBuffer`.
    *   Store the "Start Index" and "Light Count" for that froxel in a 3D `LookupTexture`.

#### B. Muzzle Flash Lifecycle
1.  Weapon system calls `RequestMuzzleFlash()` upon projectile spawn.
2.  `LightingManager` adds a point light at the muzzle bone with a `TTL = 0.05s`.
3.  Every frame, `LightingManager` decrements TTL by `DeltaTime`.
4.  If `TTL <= 0`, the light is removed from the active list before the Culling Phase begins.

#### C. Shading (Forward Pass)
1.  For every fragment (pixel) being rendered:
    *   Calculate the fragment's depth and screen-space UV.
    *   Determine which Froxel the fragment belongs to using the `LookupTexture`.
    *   Fetch the list of Light Indices for that Froxel.
    *   Loop through the specific lights, calculating the PBR (Physically Based Rendering) contribution for each.
    *   Output final color.

### 6. Implementation Logic (Example HLSL Cluster Lookup)

```cpp
// Example of how a pixel shader fetches lights from the cluster
float4 ApplyClusteredLighting(float3 worldPos, float4 screenPos) {
    // 1. Calculate Cluster Index
    uint3 clusterCoords = GetClusterCoords(screenPos);
    uint clusterIdx = GetFlatIndex(clusterCoords);

    // 2. Get Light Offset and Count from the Lookup Texture
    uint2 lightData = _ClusterLookup[clusterIdx]; 
    uint offset = lightData.x;
    uint count = lightData.y;

    float3 finalIrradiance = 0;

    // 3. Loop only through lights in this cluster
    for (uint i = 0; i < count; i++) {
        uint lightIdx = _LightIndexBuffer[offset + i];
        LightData light = _AllLights[lightIdx];
        
        finalIrradiance += CalculatePBR(light, worldPos);
    }
    
    return float4(finalIrradiance, 1.0);
}
```

*   **Then** the volume should be at the "Minimum Audible Threshold" (e.g., -40dB)
*   **And** high-frequency components of the sound should be significantly rolled off to simulate air absorption.

**Scenario: Threat entering melee range**
*   **Given** the "Hormagaunt" enemy moves from 20 meters to 2 meters away
*   **When** the enemy screeches
*   **Then** the volume should increase logarithmically following a custom inverse-square curve
*   **And** the audio should transition to a "dry" signal with high clarity and no air absorption filtering
*   **And** the controller should trigger a haptic pulse synchronized with the peak amplitude of the audio.

---

### User Story 3: Environmental Occlusion and Obstruction
**As a** Space Marine,
**I want** sounds to be muffled or redirected when blocked by walls or heavy blast doors,
**So that** I can distinguish between a threat in my current corridor and one in an adjacent room.

#### Acceptance Criteria
**Scenario: Threat behind a closed blast door**
*   **Given** there is a thick "Promethium Blast Door" between the player and a "Tyranid Warrior"
*   **When** the Tyranid Warrior roars
*   **Then** the audio system should perform a raycast check and detect the obstruction
*   **And** a Low-Pass Filter (LPF) should be applied, cutting frequencies above 500Hz
*   **And** the overall gain should be reduced by 15dB to simulate material density.

**Scenario: Threat around a corner (Diffraction)**
*   **Given** the player is in an L-shaped corridor
*   **And** a threat is around the corner with no direct line-of-sight
*   **When** the threat makes a sound
*   **Then** the audio should appear to originate from the "opening" of the corridor (the corner) rather than through the wall
*   **And** the sound should have increased reverb to simulate bouncing off the metallic corridor walls.

## 4. Key Components/Classes

### A. SpatialAudioManager (Singleton)
The central orchestrator. It manages the pool of active sound instances and performs "Audio Culling." With 300 enemies, it prioritizes the 32 closest or most dangerous threats for full HRTF processing, while downmixing distant threats to 2D/Mono.

### B. OcclusionEngine
Handles raycasting between the `ListenerPosition` and `ActiveAudioSources`. It uses a tiered update frequency:
- **Tier 1 (0-10m):** Raycast every 2 frames.
- **Tier 2 (10-30m):** Raycast every 10 frames.
- **Tier 3 (30m+):** No raycast (assumed obstructed or culled).

### C. AttenuationProcessor
Calculates the volume and frequency roll-off. It applies a Low-Pass Filter (LPF) based on the `AirAbsorptionFactor` and `MaxDistance`.

### D. HapticSynthesizer
Monitors the `ThreatProximityLevel`. When a high-priority threat (e.g., Hormagaunt) enters the "Melee Range" (defined in User Story 2), it sends a vibration buffer to the game controller synchronized with the audio's amplitude envelope.

## 5. Logic Flow

### Spatialization & Occlusion Algorithm
The following logic is executed for every high-priority audio source per update tick.

```csharp
// Logic for processing a single spatial threat sound
void ProcessThreatAudio(AudioSource source, PlayerListener listener) {
    float distance = Vector3.Distance(source.Position, listener.Position);
    
    // 1. Distance Culling
    if (distance > source.MaxDistance) {
        source.Stop();
        return;
    }

    // 2. Volume Attenuation (Custom Inverse-Square)
    float volume = CalculateLogarithmicFalloff(distance, source.MaxDistance);
    source.SetVolume(volume);

    // 3. Occlusion Check (Raycast)
    bool isObstructed = Physics.Raycast(listener.Position, (source.Position - listener.Position), out hit);
    if (isObstructed && hit.collider.tag == "Environment") {
        // Apply muffle (Low Pass Filter)
        float cutoff = MapRange(hit.material.density, 0, 1, 20000, 500); 
        source.SetLPFCutoff(cutoff);
        source.SetVolume(volume * 0.5f); // 15dB reduction approx
    } else {
        // Clear muffle (Air Absorption only)
        float airFilter = CalculateAirAbsorption(distance);
        source.SetLPFCutoff(airFilter);
    }

    // 4. HRTF Panning
    Vector3 relativePos = listener.Transform.InverseTransformPoint(source.Position);
    source.SetPan(relativePos.x); // Simplified representation of HRTF panning
    
    // 5. Haptics for Melee Range
    if (distance < 2.0f) {
        TriggerHapticPulse(source.CurrentAmplitude, distance);
    }
}
```

## 6. Dependencies

- **Physics System:** Required for raycasting to detect walls/doors for occlusion.
- **AI System:** Must provide the `Position` and `ActionType` (scuttle, roar, footstep) of the 300-enemy horde.
- **Level Design (WFC):** Modular tiles must have "Acoustic Metadata" assigned to prefabs (e.g., metal walls vs. glass windows).
- **Haptic API:** Platform-specific API (XInput/PS5 DualSense) for proximity-based vibration.
- **GPU Instancing System:** The audio system must hook into the GPU-instanced "Horde Manager" to receive batch updates on enemy positions without overhead.

SCOPE CRITICAL REVIEW
----------------------------------------

FEATURE: 300-ENEMY SWARM ARCHITECTURE (MACRO/MICRO AI)
SEVERITY: High

CRITIQUE:
Managing the transition between macro flow-fields and micro NavMesh agents for 300 entities is a massive technical debt trap.

SUGGESTION:
Reduce the active 'combat' AI to 30-40 agents and use a 2.5D or simplified vertex-animated 'background' swarm for the visual mass that doesn't require pathfinding or hitboxes.

REASONING:
For a team of 1-5, the 'promotion' logic where an entity switches from a flow-field to a NavMesh agent will create 'popping' artifacts and complex state-sync bugs. The CPU cost of 300 active hitboxes and local avoidance behaviors will tank performance on the baseline GTX 1060.

-------------------

FEATURE: HYBRID PROCEDURAL GENERATION (GRAPH-BASED + WFC)
SEVERITY: High

CRITIQUE:
Wave Function Collapse (WFC) in 3D is notoriously difficult to debug and prone to 'contradiction' errors where levels fail to generate.

SUGGESTION:
Switch to a 'Snap-to-Socket' modular room system with a fixed layout 'spine' and randomized 'Hero Room' placements.

REASONING:
WFC requires an immense amount of metadata tagging for every tile side. If one tile is tagged incorrectly, the whole generator breaks. A small team cannot afford the weeks of debugging required to ensure 100% solvable level seeds across complex 3D environments.

-------------------

FEATURE: CLIENT-HOSTED NETWORKING FOR 300 ENTITIES
SEVERITY: High

CRITIQUE:
Syncing 300 server-authoritative entities over a residential 'listen-server' connection will cause massive packet loss and rubber-banding.

SUGGESTION:
Implement 'Area of Interest' (AoI) networking where only the 10-15 enemies closest to a player are networked, while others are handled via deterministic client-side simulation.

REASONING:
Standard home upload speeds cannot handle the upstream bandwidth required to replicate 300 entity transforms and states at 20Hz. Without dedicated servers, the host's CPU will also choke on the combined burden of physics, AI, and networking.

-------------------

FEATURE: VISCERAL GORE AND DISMEMBERMENT SYSTEM
SEVERITY: Medium

CRITIQUE:
High-fidelity dismemberment is a content-creation black hole requiring unique 'damaged' versions of every enemy model.

SUGGESTION:
Use a generic 'gib' system where enemies explode into a shared pool of 5-10 blood-covered meshes rather than unique per-limb dismemberment.

REASONING:
Creating 10 enemy types is manageable. Creating 10 enemies with unique 'severed arm,' 'severed leg,' and 'exposed rib' variants triples the character art budget and increases draw calls due to multiple sub-meshes.

-------------------

FEATURE: MULTI-PLATFORM LAUNCH (PC, PS5, XSX)
SEVERITY: Medium

CRITIQUE:
Simultaneous launch on three platforms is a certification and optimization nightmare for a tiny team.

SUGGESTION:
Focus exclusively on PC (Steam) for Early Access. Use the revenue and player feedback to fund the console ports and outsourcing for certification.

REASONING:
Each console has specific 'Technical Requirements' (TRCs/XRRs) that can take months to clear. A 5-person team will spend more time fixing controller UI bugs and memory leaks for Sony/Microsoft than actually making the game fun.

-------------------

FEATURE: CLUSTERED FORWARD SHADING + HYBRID GI
SEVERITY: Low

CRITIQUE:
Building a custom rendering pipeline for high-frequency dynamic lights is over-engineering when modern engines provide these out of the box.

SUGGESTION:
Use standard Unreal Engine 5 (Lumen/Nanite) or Unity (HDRP) features rather than building a custom Clustered Forward implementation.

REASONING:
Unless the team includes a dedicated Senior Rendering Engineer, building and maintaining a custom lighting pipeline will lead to 'engine dev' paralysis where no actual gameplay progress is made for months.

-------------------
